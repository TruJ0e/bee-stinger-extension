The Guide

Demand

conceptEconomics

Demand is the quantity of a good that buyers are willing and able to purchase at a given price.

  • For example, when the price of coffee went from three dollars to five dollars at the campus cafe, daily sales dropped by almost half.
Apply it: Explain how Demand would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Law of Demand

conceptEconomics

The law of demand is the principle that, all else equal, quantity demanded falls as price rises.

Apply it: Explain how Law of Demand would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Supply

conceptEconomics

Supply is the quantity of a good that producers are willing to sell at a given price.

  • For example, when coffee prices rose, two new roasters entered the market because selling coffee became more profitable.
Apply it: Explain how Supply would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Equilibrium

conceptEconomics

Equilibrium is the price at which the quantity buyers want exactly equals the quantity sellers offer.

Apply it: Explain how Equilibrium would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Shortage

conceptEconomics

A shortage is the situation where quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.

  • For instance, during the shortage last spring, the bookstore ran out of calculators in the first week.
Apply it: Explain how Shortage would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Surplus

conceptEconomics

A surplus is the situation where quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price.

Apply it: Explain how Surplus would show up in a real situation, in your own words.

Elasticity

conceptEconomics

Elasticity is a measure of how strongly quantity demanded or supplied responds to a change in price.

  • For example, demand for insulin is nearly unchanging when its price rises, while demand for concert tickets falls quickly.
Apply it: Explain how Elasticity would show up in a real situation, in your own words.
